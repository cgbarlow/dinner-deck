# Council Report

Brief: review every collection in Iris, set criteria for an interesting, novel, surprising, delightful, engaging, interactive, dynamic exploration, score each collection on its best concept, and mock up the top five.

Scope reviewed: 14 collections, 50 sets. Collection and set metadata read in full. Sampled in detail: Barlow Family (13 diagrams, the open hypothesis document), Day to day living (43 recipes, 13 grocery aisles, recipe markup), chrisbarlow.nz (Portfolio themes and projects), NZ Government DoView packages (22), NZQA programme view, NZ AI Interest Group retrospective. Not opened diagram by diagram: the 881 DoView diagrams, SparxEA, GEA-NZ, unsorry, Contoso, Sandbox.

## Animal Perspectives

### Bear — Vision & Direction
- The collections split into two kinds: things you made about your own life and work (family, dinner, NZQA, unsorry, the website) and reference imports (DoView, SparxEA, GEA-NZ). The first kind makes better explorations because there is a person who cares what the answer is.
- The best concept per collection should do something the Iris canvas cannot: move through time, respond to a change, or search across maps.
- Barlow Family is the flagship. It has 214 people, eight generations, and an unsolved question with real evidence attached.
- NZQA is the one with career stakes. A board you can flip switches on is a better pitch than a static programme view.

### Cat — Risk & Scope
- The family tree holds living people. Any exploration needs a rule: living people stay off shared views.
- The River assumes person records carry dates and places. Dates clearly exist for some lines. Finch, Carter, Watkins and Klos start dates were not checked.
- Strategy Observatory scores high on surprise but the surprise is unproven. Nobody has yet searched 2,774 elements for shared outcomes. It may find little.
- Three collections have no elements at all (AI Budget, Quest-based Experience, NZ AI Interest Group). Their content is in diagrams and prose, which is harder to drive an interaction from.
- Scores are judgement from metadata and samples, not measurement.

### Owl — Process & Structure
- Seven criteria, one per adjective in the brief, each with a plain test. Scored 1 to 5, 35 maximum.
- Each collection scored on one named concept so the ranking compares like with like.
- Two ties: NZQA and unsorry on 29, SparxEA and chrisbarlow.nz on 23, Iris system and NZ AI Interest Group on 20.
- Sensible build order is by readiness, not rank: Dinner Deck first (data already structured, aggregation already works), then Unlock Board (edges already labelled), then The River.

### Puppy — Opportunities & Momentum
- Dinner Deck is the quick win. The recipes already point at grocery elements with quantities, and the pork shoulder already turns into lasagne.
- The open case file is the best single idea on the table. A family tree that shows its own doubt is rare.
- Swarm Theatre has a "Break something" button. That alone is worth building.
- Name Knockout for the eight candidate group names would take an afternoon and could run at the next meetup.

### Rabbit — Resources & Stakeholders
- Family: the stakeholders are relatives, and the Polish lines have their own audience.
- NZQA: the audience is NZQA leadership. Public sources only, so it can be shown.
- DoView: the maps are Dr Paul Duignan's work. An exploration built on them should credit him and probably involve him.
- unsorry: the Agentics NZ community is the audience and a source of live data the Iris model does not hold.
- Missing: place data confirmation for the family tree, and live swarm events for the theatre.

### Wolf — Cohesion & Balance
- Most attention has gone to the big imports. The small personal collections score higher per element.
- Quest-based Experience missed the top five on thin data, yet the quest idea runs through NZQA, the website and Guild Hall. It could be the wrapper for the others rather than its own exploration.
- Sandbox and Contoso are test material. Leave them out.
- GEA-NZ has 601 elements and the lowest novelty. A sunburst is what everyone would build.

## Consensus

The strongest explorations come from data with a person attached and a question still open. Barlow Family leads because it has both. Day to day living is the most buildable. NZQA has the highest stakes. unsorry is the most dynamic. DoView has the biggest possible surprise and the biggest chance of none.

Tension: surprise against certainty. The Observatory could be the best or the emptiest. A one-hour search across the NZ Government set would settle it before any build.

## Recommended Next Steps

1. Build Dinner Deck first. The data is ready and it gets used every week.
2. Run the one-hour test on Strategy Observatory: search the NZ Government set for outcomes shared across agencies. Build only if it finds something.
3. Confirm the family tree records carry dates and places for all seven lines, then set the living-people rule before building The River.
4. Build Unlock Board from the existing relationship labels in set 3 of the NZQA collection.
5. Decide whether Swarm Theatre replays the model or reads live swarm events.
